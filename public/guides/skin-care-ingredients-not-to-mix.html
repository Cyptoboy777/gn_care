Ingredient guide

Ingredients that clash, and ones that pair well

Most conflicts are about irritation, not danger. The simplest fix is to separate actives by time of day or by night.

PairWhat to doWhy
Retinol + AHA or BHAAlternate nightsBoth can dry and irritate when stacked.
Retinol + benzoyl peroxideSeparate morning and nightBenzoyl peroxide can break down some retinoids, such as tretinoin.
Vitamin C + retinolVitamin C in the morning, retinol at nightDifferent ideal conditions and both can sting.
Vitamin C + benzoyl peroxideAvoid togetherBenzoyl peroxide oxidizes vitamin C and weakens it.
AHA + BHAOne per nightEasy to over-exfoliate.
Vitamin C + niacinamideFine togetherThe old warning about this pair is outdated.
Niacinamide + retinolGood pairNiacinamide can soften retinol irritation.
Hyaluronic acid + most thingsGood pairAdds hydration that helps skin tolerate actives.

A simple schedule

  • Morning: vitamin C, moisturizer, sunscreen.
  • Night, day 1: retinoid.
  • Night, day 2: exfoliating acid, only if your skin tolerates it.
  • Night, day 3: rest with just cleanser and moisturizer.

Why strength and formula matter

Concentration, product formulation and your skin barrier all change how an ingredient feels. A gentle formula can work in combinations that a strong one cannot. If skin is stinging, peeling or tight, cut back to cleanser, moisturizer and sunscreen for a week.

Sunscreen with acids and retinoids

Acids and retinoids can make skin more sensitive to the sun. Wear sunscreen every morning when you use them.

General information, not medical advice. Prescription retinoids and acne treatments should be used as directed by your doctor.
