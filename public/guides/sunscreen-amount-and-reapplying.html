Sun care guide

How much sunscreen to use and when to reapply

The best sunscreen is one you will use in the right amount, every day.

How much to use

For the face and neck, a common guide is about a quarter to half a teaspoon. The two-finger method is an easy way to measure: squeeze a line of sunscreen along your index and middle fingers and apply it all. Most people use far less than this, which lowers the protection they actually get.

What SPF and PA mean

  • SPF measures protection against UVB, the rays that burn. SPF 30 blocks roughly 97 percent of UVB and SPF 50 roughly 98 percent, so higher numbers add a small gain.
  • Broad spectrum means it also protects against UVA, which causes ageing and pigmentation.
  • PA+ to PA++++ is a UVA rating common on Asian products. More plus signs means stronger UVA protection.

When to reapply

  • Every two hours when you are outdoors in direct sun.
  • After swimming, heavy sweating or towel drying.
  • Indoors, one morning application is usually enough unless you sit by a bright window or go outside.

Make it easier to keep

  • Pick a texture you like, such as a gel, a fluid or a stick for reapplying over makeup.
  • Put it where you will see it, next to your moisturizer.
  • Check the UV index in your weather app. At moderate levels and above, add a hat and sunglasses.
General information, not medical advice. Check your sunscreen label for its own directions, and see a doctor about any changing mole or lasting skin problem.
